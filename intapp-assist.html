Intapp Assist

When ChatGPT launched, leadership wanted an AI assistant that could answer anything about a firm's DealCloud data. I tested that idea with users before we built it. Nobody wanted it. So I worked with product and AI partners to find a problem AI could actually solve, and designed the chat experience that came out of it.

Role
Lead designer
Focus
AI strategy & conversational UX
Discipline
Web design
Year
2023
The problem

After ChatGPT came out, every enterprise company felt pressure to add generative AI, and Intapp was no different. DealCloud is a CRM for financial and legal firms, full of each firm's own data. Leadership pictured an assistant where you could ask about that data and get an answer right away:

  • "I'll be in Boston next week. Who should I reach out to?"
  • "I'm hosting a golf tournament. Find relevant contacts from our relationship history."
  • "Show me all active opportunities in a particular market."

The goal made sense: let people get to their data without learning DealCloud's structure. But there was a big gap between the idea and what our technology could do.

The question wasn't how to bring AI into DealCloud. It was where AI could actually help.

Challenge

Every firm sets up DealCloud differently. One calls it a "Deal," another an "Opportunity," another an "Engagement." Even something as simple as location can live in different custom fields.

To answer questions like the ones above, the AI needed a map of what each firm's fields mean. DealCloud didn't have that yet, and building it would take much longer than our release timeline.

So we had two things to figure out: what we could realistically ship, and, more importantly, whether users even wanted this.

The question

Before we build an AI assistant for everything, do people actually want it, and where would it really help them?

First prototype: testing the idea

Instead of debating the concept in meetings, I built a chatbot prototype. Since the AI couldn't reliably understand any question about any firm's data, I limited it to a set of questions you could click, each with a ready answer.

I partnered with a user researcher to test it with customers.

The feedback was clear: none of the users we tested wanted to use it. A chat box on top of things DealCloud could already do wasn't adding value.

What I noticed

Instead of pushing the original vision forward, I brought the findings back to our product partners. With our Product Manager, AI Engineer and AI Product Manager, I looked for a smaller problem AI could solve well with the technology we had.

  • The answers already existed. DealCloud University had lots of learning content: video tutorials, training courses, certifications and documentation.
  • Finding them took too long. Most of it was video, so getting one answer could mean watching a lot of content. 7 out of 10 users told us it took too long to find what they needed.
  • People wanted search, not more training. They expected something closer to Google: ask a question, find the right information, get a useful answer fast.
Evaluation

That gave us two directions to compare:

Help people find answers in DealCloud's knowledge Chosen
Pros

A clear user need, content that already existed, and a scope our AI could handle well today.

Cons

Narrower. It answers "how do I…" questions, not questions about the firm's own data.

An assistant for all of the firm's CRM data
Pros

The big vision. Ask anything about your firm's relationships and deals.

Cons

It needed a field-mapping layer we didn't have, it couldn't ship on time, and users in testing didn't want it.

Using the prototype, the research and the technical limits together, I helped get leadership on board to change direction.

Final solution

DealCloud had never had a chat experience, so I designed Assist's chat as a set of reusable pieces that other AI features could use later. The work focused on three things.

Conversational interaction

Assist opens from the help menu (the ? icon) in the top bar, next to University, Community and Support Portal, so it's there on every page. Asking a question and reading the answer should feel as simple as a search box.

I also explored where the chat should launch from. In one exploration it opened from the homepage, and the users I tested thought it looked like a support bot that couldn't do much. We didn't want to promise more than Assist could do. Its job was to help people find information in DealCloud's help content, not to answer any question they had. That's why it lives in the help menu, next to the other help resources.

Source attribution

Every answer lists where it came from: a video, a course lesson, a help article. People can check the answer and jump to the original when they want more.

Progressive disclosure

Answers from training content can get long. I added "Show more" inside the chat bubble, so people can scan the short version first and open the rest only when they need it.

Impact

Assist changed the direction of our first AI project. Instead of rushing a broad chatbot out without checking it with users, we used research and technical limits to find a focused problem worth solving.

It also set up what came next. For Natural Language Filter, I reused and grew the same chat patterns, this time to turn plain-language requests into filters people can check and change.

The chat patterns from Assist became the base for later AI work in DealCloud.

Reflection

Just because AI can do something doesn't mean users need it. My job wasn't to turn an executive's vision into screens. It was to help the company find out if that vision solved a real problem.

Sometimes the most useful design decision is knowing what not to build.
